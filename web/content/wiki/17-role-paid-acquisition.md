# Role guide: Paid Acquisition (requesting ads)

**Who this is for:** anyone on Paid Acquisition who needs ads made. Your whole job in ADAM is the
order form. You don't run the tool, you don't drive the gates, and you don't touch Figma. A
copywriter and a designer pick it up from there.

**Time:** about five minutes per request.

---

## What you're doing

You're writing a request. ADAM already has Upwork's brand voice, the legal rules, the approved
claims, and the ads that have performed. What it doesn't have is what this batch is for. That's the
part you supply.

---

## Filling out the form

Three steps.

### 1. Details

- **Requested by.** Your name. It shows on the sprint so people know whose request it is.
- **Audience.** Prospecting, Retargeting, or Both. "Both" gives you a separate version of every ad
  for each audience.
- **Delivery date.** When you need them. Five business days out, minimum.
- **Deliverable.** Almost always "Images & Copy".
- **Platform.** Meta, LinkedIn, Reddit, YouTube, Google/Bing, or 3rd Party.

A note on "Both". You get two genuinely independent ads. They share a look, not a message.
Prospecting talks to people who've never used Upwork. Retargeting talks to people who already
visited. They don't have to be about the same thing.

### 2. Creative

Pick your ad styles and sizes. Each style is a different visual layout and the picker shows you a
thumbnail. There's a full list further down with how much copy each one holds.

**Ask for about five ads.** This is the most useful thing on this page. Quality holds up at four to
six. Past that, the concepts start repeating themselves. If you need twelve, do two requests of six.

It's worth knowing what you're actually asking for. Five ads means ADAM writes six concepts for each
of your five styles, picks the best of each, then builds every size you ticked, for each audience.
Five is already a lot of output.

### 3. Brief

Everything else on the form is a dropdown. The brief is where you actually make decisions, and it's
the highest priority instruction ADAM gets. It overrides the standing reference docs.

Hit **Insert brief template** and fill in the four sections:

- **THEME.** One or two sentences. The core message every ad should lead with.
- **COPY MUST-DOs.** Required phrases, claims to feature, tone notes, things to stay away from.
- **DESIGN DIRECTION.** Visual or format cues. Put "N/A" if you don't have any.
- **RESOURCES.** Links, reference ads, approved stats, badge assets.

**Don't submit a blank brief.** ADAM will warn you and let you through, but you'll get generic
evergreen copy written off the standing brand docs with no angle to it. If you cancel the warning to
go back and write one, that's fine. Add it and submit again.

---

## Writing a brief that works

A good brief names a theme, says what you're testing, and hands over the actual proof points and
example copy you want mined. A weak one names a topic and stops.

**The KOTH briefs are the model.** Go pull one of those and match that level of detail. That's a
better guide than anything this page could describe.

A couple of things that help. Put claims and stats in RESOURCES rather than writing them from
memory, because ADAM uses what you give it and the legal screening runs against the approved docs.
And if the batch is a test, say what the test is. Naming the hypothesis gets you a sharper set than
naming the topic.

## How much copy actually fits

Every style has a real character budget and the tool enforces it. So picking a style is partly a
decision about how much you get to say.

If you've got a lot to communicate, go for the styles with room. If your message is one sharp line,
a small style will serve it better than a big layout with padding in it.

### Meta ad types (24)

- **Split Screen.** A headline over two side by side images. Headline only, no body copy. 65
  characters, or 49 on the version that carries a CTA.
- **Us vs Them.** Split vertically, illustrated, two positions against each other. Two headlines at
  18 each, three bullets a side at 28 each.
- **Photo with Text (Upwork shell).** A branded photo of a professional inside an Upwork border.
  Headline 24, subhead 43, CTA 19.
- **Lifestyle Photo (Full Bleed).** Photography fills the frame and copy sits over it. Headline 100.
  The most room of any photo style.
- **Testimonial.** A real client quote with stars or badges. Quote 100, attribution 51. The quotes
  come from an approved list inside the tool.
- **Social Media Profile.** A mocked up freelancer profile with headshot, name and job title. Name
  18, title 28, two side fields at 60.
- **Pie Chart.** A circular chart in four quadrants. Quadrant labels 30, center callout 28.
- **Hybrid.** Lifestyle photography mixed with Upwork UI elements. Headline 36, subhead 60, CTA 22.
- **Search Results.** Three search bars with role tags and hire buttons. Headline 38, each result 32.
- **Text Only.** Text driven, on a branded background. Headline 20, subhead 60, CTA 22.
- **Chat Bubble.** Two chat bubbles playing out a conversation. Label 18, message 91. Write it as an
  actual exchange, not two slogans.
- **Notification.** Looks like an app notification. Headline 52, CTA 12.
- **Reminder.** A mobile pop up reminder. Headline 52, CTA 20.
- **Device UI (Photo).** A phone or laptop showing the Upwork platform. Headline 38.
- **Platform UI.** A moment from the product, like the homepage, search, or Uma. Headline 25, subhead
  18, CTA 16.
- **Meme.** A viral format carrying the message. Headline 43.
- **Sticky Note.** A note with a title and items listed under it, single or two columns. Headlines 12
  a side, bullets 30.
- **Poll.** A poll bar over a background image. Question 75, two options at 14 each, CTA 12.
- **Tweet / Post Mockup.** Reads like an organic social post. 115 characters, the most text of
  anything in the set.
- **Text with Button and Cursor.** A UI ad with a button and a cursor clicking it. Button text 15.
- **Talent Profile.** A freelancer profile card with a headline on top. Headline 36, CTA 21.
- **Bespoke.** Custom layout, no fixed structure. No caps and no template, so this one needs a
  designer involved.
- **Illustration.** Illustration tied to the theme. Headline 38, CTA 16.
- **Carousel.** A multi slide ad. Headline 36, CTA 22.

### Reddit ad types (20)

Reddit runs tighter than Meta. A lot of these mirror a Meta type at a smaller size and the copy
budget shrinks with it. If you're ordering the same concept for both, expect the Reddit version to
say less. Reddit comes in two sizes, 1080x1350 portrait and 1440x1080 landscape.

- **Meme.** Viral formats with the message on them. Headline 45.
- **Graphic With Text.** A maze graphic showing the easy path to hiring. Two headlines at 12 each.
  Very tight.
- **Split Screen.** Two or more photos on opposite sides with copy. Headline 49, CTA 14. Mirrors the
  Meta Split Screen.
- **Pie Chart.** A segmented round graph. Labels 20, center 28. Mirrors Meta Pie Chart with less copy.
- **App Notification.** An inbox icon with a headline call to action. Headline 45.
- **Text Only.** Mostly text. Headline 20, subhead 30, CTA 18. The CTA is optional and built in but
  hidden. Mirrors Meta Text Only with less room.
- **Person With Text.** Text plus a person profile. Headline 30, CTA 15. Close to Meta Talent Profile.
- **Venn Diagram.** Two overlapping circles. Left and right labels are 7 characters, so one word
  each. Center body 38, subhead 50.
- **Person Only.** One person, full bleed or avatar style. No copy on the asset at all.
- **Us vs Them.** Opposing views on opposite sides. Left headline 22, right headline 18, bullets 60.
- **Testimonial.** A user quote with optional stars or badges. Quote 60, attribution 46. Mirrors Meta
  Testimonial with less room.
- **Button.** A branded graphic with a button and a cursor. Headline 16.
- **Search.** A search bar with the job title as the variable. Headline 26.
- **Icon.** An icon and a tagline, usually calling out a feature or a freelancer status. Headline 26.
- **Note.** A reminder note in Upwork brand colors. Headline 60.
- **Notification.** A mobile reminder on a branded background. Headline 52, CTA 20. Mirrors the Meta
  Reminder.
- **Search and Checkbox.** A checkbox search UI, with or without a headline. Headline 45, search
  field 15, list items 12.
- **Text with Icons.** Left aligned text with icons. Headline 20, list items 8 each.
- **Twitter.** A tweet mockup on a Glimmer background. 115 characters.
- **Logo.** Logo on the brand Glimmer gradient. No headline, no CTA.

The rest of the per type rules, the tone and punctuation and CTA policy, live inside the tool and get
applied on their own. You don't need to memorize any of it. This list is here so you can pick a style
knowing roughly what'll fit.

## After you submit

You're done. You'll get a sprint link. The copywriter takes it from there, reviews the copy, and the
designer assembles the ads in Figma.

If you want to follow along, the sprint page shows you where it is. You don't have to approve
anything.

### Why there's a minimum

The work is gated and the gates are people, so the lead time isn't machine time. Copy review is the
slow human step and it's the one that decides whether the batch is any good. Image generation is the
slow machine step.

Asking for a faster turnaround mostly squeezes the copy review, which is the step you least want
squeezed.

---

## When to come back

- **The theme was wrong or you want a different angle.** Submit a new request. Copy gets locked early
  so editing a sprint that's already running isn't the path.
- **You want more of the same.** New request, same brief, different styles.
- **Something looks broken.** Tell the copywriter running it. They can file it from inside the tool.
- **The copy keeps missing the same way across batches.** Say so instead of re briefing around it.
  That kind of thing can get fixed permanently in the tool's guidance, but only if somebody reports
  the pattern.

---

## If you remember three things

1. The brief is your whole contribution. Five minutes of real direction there beats every other field
   combined.
2. Ask for around five. More isn't better, and two small requests beat one big one.
3. Pick styles by how much you need to say. Those character budgets are real and they're enforced.
