# Role guide: Paid Acquisition (requesting ads)

**Owner:** Adrie Etherington · **Last updated:** 2026-09-21

---

## What is ADAM?

ADAM turns a written brief into finished ad creative. You describe what the batch is for, and it
writes the copy and builds the ads against Upwork's approved templates, with a person checking the
work at every step.

It already knows Upwork's brand voice, the legal rules, the approved claims and which past ads
performed. What it doesn't know is what *this* batch is for. That's your job.

### How the whole thing flows

```
You submit the brief  →  Copywriter reviews the copy  →  ADAM generates the images
      →  Designer builds the ads in Figma  →  You get the finished ads
```

### Your role in it

| | |
|---|---|
| **What you get** | Finished ads, in every size you asked for, for each audience |
| **What you do** | Fill in the order form. That's the whole job |
| **What you hand off** | A sprint link, created automatically when you submit |
| **Who gets it next** | The copywriter, who reviews the copy and drives it through to the designer |

You don't run the tool, drive the approvals, or touch Figma.

## Quick start

1. Open the order form.
2. **Details:** your name, the audience, the delivery date, the platform.
3. **Creative:** pick about five ad styles and the sizes you need.
4. **Brief:** hit *Insert brief template* and fill in all four sections. This is the part that
   matters.
5. Submit. Copy the sprint link and post it in the channel.

Five minutes, and then you're done until the ads come back.

## Filling out the form

### 1. Details

- **Requested by.** Your name, so people know whose request it is.
- **Audience.** Prospecting, Retargeting, or Both. "Both" produces a separate version of every ad
  for each audience, with its own copy and its own photo.
- **Delivery date.** **Ten business days out, minimum.** Ask for less and you are squeezing the copy
  review, which is the step that decides whether the batch is any good.
- **Deliverable.** Almost always "Images & Copy".
- **Platform.** See below. Meta and Reddit are supported today.

A note on "Both": the two versions are genuinely independent ads. Same style, their own copy, their
own photo. Prospecting talks to people who've never used Upwork, Retargeting to people who already
visited. They don't have to be about the same thing.

### Which platforms you can actually request

| Platform | Status |
|---|---|
| **Meta** | Supported. 24 ad types with copy rules and templates |
| **Reddit** | Supported. 20 ad types. Reddit's feed copy is one short body line, no headline |
| LinkedIn, YouTube, Google/Bing, 3rd Party | **Not yet.** Templates exist, but the ad types and copy rules haven't been defined |

For anything in the bottom row, talk to Adrie before submitting. Adrie and Elise are working through
the copy requirements for those platforms; until that lands, ADAM has no styles to offer and no
character limits to enforce, so a request will come back thin.

### 2. Creative

Pick your ad styles and sizes. Each style is a different visual layout and the picker shows a
thumbnail of each.

**Ask for about five ads.** Quality holds up at four to six. Past that, concepts start repeating
themselves. If you need twelve, two requests of six beat one of twelve.

Worth knowing what you're really asking for: five ads means ADAM writes six concepts for each of
your five styles, picks the best of each, then builds every size you ticked, for each audience.

### How to pick an ad style

Mostly it's a decision about **how much you need to say**:

- **A lot to communicate?** Tweet/Post Mockup (115 characters), Lifestyle Photo (100), Chat Bubble
  (91).
- **One sharp line?** Text Only, Split Screen, Notification, Meme.
- **Proof or credibility?** Testimonial, Talent Profile, Social Media Profile.
- **Showing the product?** Platform UI, Device UI, Search Results.
- **A comparison or data point?** Us vs Them, Pie Chart, Poll.

Full character budgets for all 44 ad types are in the reference at the bottom of this guide.

### 3. Brief

Everything else is a dropdown. The brief is where your judgment enters, and it's the highest
priority instruction ADAM gets. It overrides the standing reference docs.

Hit **Insert brief template** and fill in four sections:

- **THEME.** One or two sentences. The core message every ad should lead with.
- **COPY MUST-DOs.** Required phrases, claims to feature, tone notes, things to avoid.
- **DESIGN DIRECTION.** Visual or format cues. "N/A" if you have none.
- **RESOURCES.** Links, reference ads, approved stats, badge assets.

**Don't submit a blank brief.** ADAM will warn you and let you through, but you'll get generic
evergreen copy with no angle.

---

## What a strong brief looks like

A strong brief names a theme, says what you're testing, and hands over the actual proof points you
want used. A weak one names a topic and stops.

| | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| **THEME** | "Hiring on Upwork" | One or two sentences naming the single message, and who it's aimed at |
| **COPY MUST-DOs** | "Mention speed" | Phrases to hit, words to avoid, the tone, and what NOT to claim |
| **DESIGN DIRECTION** | "N/A" | A format preference, or an explicit "no preference" |
| **RESOURCES** | Empty | Links to the stats, the approved claims, and reference ads that worked |

**The KOTH briefs are the model.** [LINK: a recent KOTH brief that does this well]

Two things that help. Put claims and stats in RESOURCES rather than writing them from memory, since
ADAM uses what you give it and the legal screening runs against the approved docs. And if the batch
is a test, say what the test is. Naming the hypothesis gets you a sharper set than naming the topic.

## When to come back

- **The theme was wrong, or you want a different angle.** Submit a new request. Copy locks early, so
  editing a running sprint isn't the path.
- **You want more of the same.** New request, same brief, different styles.
- **Something looks broken.** Tell the copywriter running it. They can file it from inside the tool.
- **The copy keeps missing the same way across batches.** Say so rather than re-briefing around it.
  That can be fixed permanently in the tool's guidance, but only if someone reports the pattern.

---

## What you can fix yourself, and what you can't

| You can | Somebody else has to |
|---|---|
| Rewrite the brief and resubmit | Change an ad type's character limits (engineer) |
| Change the styles, sizes or audience | Add a new ad style (designer builds the template first) |
| Ask for a different angle | Fix how the copy reads across every batch (copywriter, via the tool's guidance) |
| Report something that looks wrong | Anything in Figma |

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sprint** | One run through ADAM. Your request becomes a sprint |
| **Gate** | A checkpoint where a person approves before it continues. There are six |
| **Concept** | One generated copy option. ADAM writes six per style and keeps the best |
| **Visual style** | The ad type and layout, like Testimonial or Pie Chart |
| **Template** | The Figma frame a style is built from |
| **Assembly** | The designer's step, building the finished ads in Figma |
| **Manifest** | The file listing every ad, its copy and its image, handed to the designer |
| **Learning** | A standing instruction that changes how ADAM writes on every future run |
| **Issue** | A problem logged inside the tool so it gets tracked and fixed |

Fuller list in the [Glossary](13-glossary.md).

---

## Who to ask

- **Copy rules, tone, claims, approved quotes** → the creative lead
- **Templates, layouts, how an ad looks in Figma** → the designer
- **A sprint that's stuck or errored** → the copywriter running it
- **A style that doesn't exist yet** → the designer builds it first, then it can go on the form

---

## The three things worth remembering

1. **The brief is your whole contribution.** Five minutes of real direction beats every other field
   combined.
2. **Ask for around five.** More isn't better, and two small requests beat one big one.
3. **Pick styles by how much you need to say.** The character budgets are real and enforced.
