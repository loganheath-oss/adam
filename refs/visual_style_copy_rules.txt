UPWORK AD TYPE STYLE GUIDE — Text_On_Visual Copy Rules by Ad Type
==================================================================
BINDING reference for the Upwork Meta Ad Copywriter project (Adrie Etherington,
last updated July 2026). Before writing any on-creative (Text_On_Visual) copy,
identify the ad type from Ad_Format + Visual_Style, locate the matching entry
below, and apply its rules EXACTLY. These rules override all general
Text_On_Visual defaults.

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE
- Match the brief's Visual_Style to an entry below.
- Apply every rule in that entry — character limits, sentence count, punctuation,
  layout density, and CTA guidance.
- Note the matched entry in the brief summary line
  (e.g., "Ad type resolved to: Chat Bubble — applying Style Guide entry #11").
- If no match is found, apply the closest entry and flag the mismatch. Do not
  silently default to a generic single-headline spec.

GENERAL RULES (apply to ALL entries)
- Follow the Upwork brand voice: clear, concise, supportive, professional.
- 95/5 rule: 95% informative/helpful, 5% subtle personality.
- Use only approved claims and verified statistics.
- Sentence case. No trailing periods on headlines or Text_On_Visual by default.
- Character limits below are editorial intent; where a Figma template slot has a
  tighter HARD cap (see the TEMPLATE CHARACTER LIMITS block in the assignment),
  that cap wins — always fit the slot.


Entry 1 — Split Screen
Layout: A headline at the top above two side-by-side images. The headline is the
only copy element — no body copy, no text on the images. The visual does the work.
Density: Low — one line only.
Rules:
- Write one short, punchy headline. This is the only copy field for this ad type.
- No subhead. No body copy. No text on images.
- Sentence case. No trailing period.
- Two versions: one with a CTA, remaining versions without.
Character limits: Headline with CTA: max 49 · Headline without CTA: max 65 · CTA: max 14
Punctuation: Minimal. Em dashes allowed. No exclamation points. Question marks only if the concept calls for one.
Tone: The headline frames the two images — write as if completing the image's thought.
CTA: Optional. One variant includes a CTA; the rest do not.


Entry 2 — Us vs Them
Layout: Split vertically in an illustrated style. Left = "us" (Upwork/freelancers),
right = "them" (the alternative). Each side: a short headline, 2–3 bullet proof
points, and a short wrap-up line. Extends to ways of working, payment, hiring, etc.
Density: Medium-High.
Rules:
- Headline for each side (left and right).
- 2–3 bullets per side — short, factual, parallel in structure across both sides.
- A one-liner wrap-up beneath the bullets on each side.
- Sentence case. No trailing periods on headlines. Bullets may end without punctuation.
- No CTA on visual unless the brief explicitly requests one.
Character limits: Left headline: max 18 · Right headline: max 18 · Total bullet text per side: max 176 · Left wrap-up: max 31 · Right wrap-up: max 31
Punctuation: Bullets fragment-style, no periods. Wrap-up lines may use an em dash. No exclamation points.
Tone: "Us" = confident, outcome-focused. "Them" = factual, neutral — never mocking. Let the contrast speak.


Entry 3 — Photo with Text (Upwork shell)
Layout: A branded photo of a professional in an Upwork border. A short headline in
the lower left. The photo does the emotional work; the headline anchors the message.
Density: Low — headline only, or headline + short subhead when no CTA.
Rules:
- Write a headline. Short, outcome-led, sentence case, no trailing period.
- Two versions: one with a CTA, remaining versions without.
- When no CTA is used, a subhead may expand the message.
Character limits: Headline (all versions): max 24 · CTA: max 19 · Subhead (no-CTA only): max 43
Punctuation: Minimal. Land cleanly without punctuation where possible.
Tone: Warm and direct. The photo carries the human element — keep the headline crisp and confident.
CTA: Optional. One variant includes a CTA; the rest do not.


Entry 4 — Lifestyle Photo (Full Bleed)
Layout: Full-bleed branded photography — the photo fills the frame, copy sits over it.
Room for a longer headline than most photo formats, but keep density in check.
Density: Low-Medium.
Rules:
- One copy block — a headline or short headline-plus-thought.
- Two versions: one with a CTA, remaining versions without.
- When no CTA is used, the character limit expands.
- Sentence case. No trailing period.
Character limits: Copy with CTA: max 80 · Copy without CTA: max 100 · CTA: max 19
Punctuation: A period is allowed if the copy splits into two distinct sentences. Otherwise avoid trailing punctuation.
Tone: Aspirational but grounded. Match the photo's emotional register.
CTA: Optional. One variant includes a CTA; the rest do not.


Entry 5 — Testimonial
Layout: A real client testimonial with brand elements (stars/badges). Always
includes attribution (name, job title, company).
Density: Medium — quote plus attribution line.
Rules:
- Use a real quote from attached docs or the brief. NEVER fabricate a quote.
- Quotes may be shortened but must not change the intended meaning.
- Always include: first name, last-name initial, job title, and company.
- If no real quote can be sourced and none was provided, use Lorem Ipsum and flag it.
- Sentence case for attribution. No trailing period on attribution.
Character limits: Testimonial copy: max 100 · Credit line (name, title, company): max 51
Punctuation: Opening + closing quotation marks required on the testimonial. Attribution: comma between name/title and title/company.
Tone: Let the quote speak. Do not editorialize or frame it with extra copy.
CTA: Not applicable to visual copy.


Entry 6 — Social Media Profile
Layout: A mockup of a freelancer's social profile — headshot, name (first + last
initial), job title. Left/right columns hold supporting content; a subhead below
the columns is a punchy wrap-up.
Density: Medium-High.
Rules:
- Write left and right column content (skills, outcomes, efficiency, quality, clever lines).
- Write a subhead below the columns — a punchy closer.
- Do NOT write the name or job title (design placeholders / provided).
- Sentence case. No trailing periods.
Character limits: Column left + right combined: max 65 · Subhead: max 28
Punctuation: Fragments OK in columns. Subhead may use an em dash. No exclamation points.
Tone: Clever and professional. Feel authentic, not ad-like — what a skilled freelancer would actually list.
CTA: Not applicable to visual copy.


Entry 7 — Pie Chart
Layout: A circular chart in 4 quadrants — features, benefits, outcomes, or stats.
A center callout ties the quadrants together or adds a branded anchor.
Density: Medium — four quadrant labels + one center callout.
Rules:
- Write four quadrant copy blocks — each a short, distinct point (feature/benefit/stat/outcome).
- Write one center callout (theme-aligned or a branded line, e.g. "Hire smarter").
- All four quadrants parallel in structure (all fragments, or all short phrases — don't mix).
- Sentence case. No trailing periods.
Character limits: Each quadrant: max 30 · Center callout: max 28
Punctuation: Fragments preferred. No periods. Em dashes allowed if warranted.
Tone: Informative and confident. Stats only from approved claims — do not fabricate data.
CTA: Not applicable to visual copy.


Entry 8 — Hybrid
Layout: Lifestyle photography + branded Upwork UI elements tied to the concept.
Always includes a CTA.
Density: Medium — headline, subhead, and CTA. All required.
Rules:
- Write a headline and a subhead. Headline leads; subhead extends/supports.
- Always include a CTA. There are NO no-CTA versions.
- Sentence case. No trailing period on headline or subhead.
Character limits: Headline: max 36 · Subhead: max 60 · CTA: max 22
Punctuation: A period between headline and subhead only if both are complete, distinct sentences. Otherwise avoid.
Tone: The UI grounds the ad — copy should feel like the product in action: confident, specific, outcome-led.
CTA: Required on every variant.


Entry 9 — Search Results
Layout: Three search bars with freelancer role tags inside, each with a hire button.
A headline sits above.
Density: Low-Medium — headline + three talent tags.
Rules:
- One headline. Short, outcome-led or cleverly framed. Sentence case, no trailing period.
- Three talent tags — one per bar. Realistic search terms, written as a user would type them, not marketing language. Pull from Freelancer Work Categories unless the brief specifies.
Character limits: Headline: max 38 · Each talent tag: max 32
Punctuation: No punctuation on talent tags. Headline may use an em dash if warranted.
Tone: The visual demonstrates — the headline frames the payoff cleanly. Tags feel authentic and specific.
CTA: Not specified for visual copy — follow brief or default.


Entry 10 — Text Only
Layout: Text-driven design, maybe a branded background/container. No photo or
illustration — copy carries the full weight.
Density: Medium — three fields, all tightly capped.
Rules:
- Write a headline, subhead, and CTA. All three used.
- Headline lands the core message; subhead extends/proves it; CTA closes.
- Sentence case. No trailing periods on headline or subhead.
- Copy must be especially sharp and self-contained (no visual support).
Character limits: Headline: max 20 · Subhead: max 60 · CTA: max 22
Punctuation: Fragments OK in headline. Subhead may be a complete sentence. Em dash allowed for rhythm.
Tone: Confident and direct. Every word is load-bearing — cut anything that doesn't earn its place.
CTA: Required on every variant.


Entry 11 — Chat Bubble
Layout: TWO chat bubbles simulating a text conversation between two people. One
bubble opens, the second closes. The exchange should sound like a natural
conversation — NOT ad copy, NOT a headline + subtext.
Density: Low — two short copy blocks written as DIALOGUE, not headlines.
Rules:
- Write two chat bubbles: an opening message and a reply.
- Write as a real conversation — casual, natural, direct. Not a headline. Not a tagline.
- The exchange should prove a point, answer a question, or surface a compelling stat about using freelancers or Upwork.
- Sentence case. Conversational punctuation is fine (periods mid-sentence, ellipses if natural). Avoid exclamation points unless genuinely warranted.
Character limits: Chat bubble 1 (opener): max 18 · Chat bubble 2 (reply): max 91
Punctuation: Write as texting — short, natural, human. Full stops OK. Fragments OK.
Tone: Real. Grounded. Two people who sound like actual humans, not brand voices. The format creates trust — the copy just has to not break it.
CTA: Not applicable to visual copy.


Entry 12 — Notification
Layout: An ad that looks like an app notification. Copy mimics a real notification.
Always includes a CTA.
Density: Low — headline + CTA only.
Rules:
- Write copy that reads like an ACTUAL app notification — hint at hiring a freelancer, finding talent, or a successful Upwork outcome, WITHOUT reading like an ad.
- Always include a CTA. There are NO no-CTA versions.
- Sentence case. No trailing period on the notification copy.
Character limits: Headline (notification copy): max 52 · CTA: max 12
Punctuation: Notification-style — clean and direct. A period is OK if it reads as a complete sentence. Avoid exclamation points.
Tone: Functional and believable — like something that actually popped up on a phone. If it reads like an ad, rewrite it.
CTA: Required on every variant.


Entry 13 — Reminder
Layout: A mobile app pop-up reminder. Short, direct, action-oriented. Always a CTA.
Density: Low — one copy block + CTA.
Rules:
- Write copy that reads like a real reminder notification, tied to working with freelancers, hiring on Upwork, or a specific outcome.
- Keep it short — the format demands brevity.
- Always include a CTA. There are NO no-CTA versions.
- Sentence case. Punctuation OK if it aids clarity.
Character limits: Copy: max 52 · CTA: max 20
Punctuation: A period is fine for a complete-sentence reminder. Keep it clean.
Tone: Practical and believable — like something a user set for themselves. Upwork connection feels natural, not forced.
CTA: Required on every variant.


Entry 14 — Device UI (Photo)
Layout: A device (phone/laptop) showing Upwork's platform/UI. A headline appears
at the top above the device.
Density: Low — headline only.
Rules:
- One headline, relating to the UI shown on the device.
- Sentence case. No trailing period.
- The headline frames what the viewer sees — it should make them want to look closer.
Character limits: Headline: max 38
Punctuation: Minimal. Em dash allowed if warranted.
Tone: Confident and specific. The device + UI demonstrate — the headline anchors the payoff.
CTA: Not specified for visual copy — follow brief or default.


Entry 15 — Platform UI
Layout: A featured moment from the Upwork platform (homepage, search page, Uma
interface). The headline relates to the specific UI feature. CTA optional.
Density: Low — headline + optional subhead, expanding when no CTA.
Rules:
- Write a headline and subhead, both relating to the UI feature shown.
- Two versions: one with a CTA, remaining versions without.
- When no CTA, combined room increases by 16 characters (distribute as needed).
- Sentence case. No trailing periods.
Character limits (with CTA): Headline: max 25 · Subhead: max 18 · CTA: max 16
Character limits (without CTA): +16 characters distributed between headline and subhead as needed.
Punctuation: Minimal — copy should feel like a clean label for what's shown.
Tone: Platform-native — copy belongs in the product, not on top of it.
CTA: Optional. One variant includes a CTA; the rest do not.


Entry 16 — Meme
Layout: A viral meme format carrying key messaging. Copy usually prompted or
requested in the brief.
Density: Low — headline only, but tone carries the weight.
Rules:
- Write copy that fits the meme format referenced in the brief.
- If no specific meme format is provided, flag it — meme copy structure depends on the format.
- Copy should feel native to the meme — if it reads like an ad, it fails.
- Sentence case where natural; meme formats sometimes use intentional capitalization as part of the joke — follow the format convention.
Character limits: Headline: max 43
Punctuation: Follow the meme format's conventions — format-dependent.
Tone: Witty and culturally aware. Don't force Upwork messaging into a meme awkwardly — find the angle that earns the format.
CTA: Not typically used on the visual — follow brief if specified.


Entry 17 — Sticky Note
Layout: A sticky note with a title at top and listed items below — single column
or two columns. Checklist, comparison, us-vs-them, or feature/benefit.
Density: Medium — title + column content. Layout varies by version.
Rules:
- Write a title (headline) and column content.
- Write at least one single-column and one double-column version per concept.
- Column content is list-style — short items, parallel structure. Checkmarks, dashes, or plain text depending on the concept.
- Sentence case. No trailing periods on list items.
Character limits (double-column): Headline: max 12 · Column A + B combined: max 136
Character limits (single-column): Headline: max 26 · Single column content: max 112
Punctuation: List items are fragments — no periods. Title may use minimal punctuation if warranted.
Tone: Practical and visual — like something someone actually wrote down, not a polished brand statement.
CTA: Not applicable to visual copy.


Entry 18 — Poll
Layout: A background image with a poll bar overlaid — stats, mock results, or
comparisons. Always includes a CTA.
Density: Medium — headline, subhead/stat, and CTA.
Rules:
- Write a headline and a subhead or stat line. The poll bar carries the data; the headline frames it.
- Content may be prompted by the brief or generated from approved Upwork stats.
- Always include a CTA. There are NO no-CTA versions.
- Sentence case. No trailing period on headline.
Character limits: Headline: max 36 · Subhead/stat: max 75 · CTA: max 12
Punctuation: Stats may use a colon or em dash to separate number from label. Headline stays clean.
Tone: Data-led where possible. Stats from approved claims only. A mock poll must feel believable and relevant to the SMB audience.
CTA: Required on every variant.


Entry 19 — Tweet / Post Mockup
Layout: A mockup of a tweet or social post — organic social content, a veiled ad
that reads like a real post.
Density: Medium — one copy block, moderate length.
Rules:
- Write copy that reads like a REAL social post, not an ad.
- The Upwork connection should feel natural and earned — not brand-inserted.
- Sentence case. Conversational punctuation is fine.
- Do NOT include handles, hashtags, or platform UI elements in the copy — those are design elements.
Character limits: Copy: max 115
Punctuation: Write as a real post — periods, natural line breaks, light punctuation. No exclamation points unless the voice calls for one.
Tone: Authentic and human. Lives or dies on whether it reads like something a real person would post. If it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.
CTA: Not applicable to visual copy.


Entry 20 — Text with Button and Cursor
Layout: A UI-style ad with a button element and a cursor clicking it. The copy is
INSIDE the button — the button copy is the entire message.
Density: Minimal — one copy element only.
Rules:
- Write copy for the button/CTA only. This is the sole copy field.
- Must be verb-led — it is both the message and the call to action.
- Feel like a real UI button — action-oriented and specific.
- Sentence case. No trailing period.
Character limits: CTA / button copy: max 15
Punctuation: None. Buttons don't have punctuation.
Tone: Direct and action-forward. In 15 characters, imply a benefit and prompt a click.
CTA: The button copy IS the CTA. No separate CTA field.


Entry 21 — Talent Profile
Layout: A freelancer profile card with a headline at the top. The profile is the
visual element; the headline is the primary copy.
Density: Low — headline only, plus optional CTA.
Rules:
- One headline, relating to the profile shown and framing the value of finding talent on Upwork.
- Two versions with a CTA; remaining versions (out of 6) without.
- Sentence case. No trailing period.
Character limits: Headline: max 36 · CTA: max 21
Punctuation: Minimal. Em dash allowed if warranted.
Tone: Confident and specific. The profile is the visual proof — the headline makes the viewer want to hire.
CTA: Optional. Two variants include a CTA; the rest do not.


Entry 22 — Bespoke
Layout: A custom layout built from scratch — no fixed structure.
Density: Variable — determined by the brief's described layout.
Rules:
- Copy is variable. Read the brief for layout description, visual references, creative direction.
- Identify the closest matching ad type and apply those structural rules as a starting point.
- One variant includes a CTA; the rest without, unless the brief specifies otherwise.
- Sentence case throughout. All other formatting guardrails apply.
Character limits: Text: variable — use judgment based on the layout density described in the brief.
Tone: Fully brief-dependent. Default to the core voice: clear, outcome-led, grounded.
CTA: Optional. One variant includes a CTA; the rest do not — unless the brief specifies.


Entry 23 — Illustration
Layout: Illustrations tied to the ad theme. The illustration carries the visual
concept; copy complements it.
Density: Low — headline + optional CTA.
Rules:
- One headline, connecting to the illustration theme and landing the core message.
- One variant includes a CTA; remaining versions without.
- Sentence case. No trailing period.
Character limits: Headline: max 38 · CTA: max 16
Punctuation: Minimal. Let illustration and headline work together cleanly.
Tone: Match the illustration — playful illustration, playful headline; bold/graphic illustration, tight/confident headline.
CTA: Optional. One variant includes a CTA; the rest do not.


Entry 24 — Carousel
Layout: A multi-slide ad. Each slide has its own headline. The final slide always
includes a CTA. Default 3–4 slides unless the brief specifies.
Density: Low per slide — one headline each; the narrative builds across slides.
Rules:
- Write a headline for each slide — each unique, each connected to the overall theme.
- Default output: two versions with 4 slides, one version with 3 slides (per concept).
- Longer carousels are specified in the brief with extra guidance.
- The FINAL slide must always include a CTA.
- Headlines flow as a progression — don't repeat the same thought across slides.
- Sentence case. No trailing periods.
Character limits: Each slide headline: max 36 · Final slide CTA: max 22
Punctuation: Minimal per slide. Em dashes allowed. Headlines are distinct beats, not fragments of one sentence.
Tone: Narrative and progressive — each slide makes the viewer want the next. The final slide closes and converts.
CTA: Required on the final slide only. All other slides: no CTA.
