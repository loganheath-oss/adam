Heading 
Style and format 
 
Body copy 
When we're consistent in our writing style, it helps us build trust with the people who interact 
with Upwork every day. It helps them see that we're one company, no matter which internal 
team wrote the thing they're reading.  
 
This guide covers the basics that are specific to Upwork. If your case isn’t covered, use AP 
style.

Formatting 
 
Body copy 
Abbreviations 
It’s okay to use most standard American abbreviations, including acronyms (within reason), 
shortened words, and contractions.  
For acronyms, spell out the full term on first use, followed by the abbreviation in parentheses. 
After that, you can use the acronym on its own. 
Periods with abbreviations 
We don’t use periods in many abbreviations, especially in measurements, time units, states, 
and well-known acronyms. Refer to AP style for a full list.  
✅ Do this: 
Job time: 20 min 
Monica is based in New York, NY. 
The CEO presented the company’s growth strategy during the annual meeting. 
❌Not this: 
Job time: 20 min. 
She works out of Washington, D.C. 
The C.E.O. presented the company’s growth strategy during the annual meeting. 
 
One important exception is to use periods for the abbreviation of the United States. 
✅ Do this: 
This freelancer lives in the U.S. 
❌Not this: 
This company is looking for US-based freelancers. 
 
File types and extensions 
Use all caps for abbreviations of file types. 
✅ Do this: 
You can upload JPEG, GIF, or PNG files. 
❌Not this: 
You can upload .jpeg, .gif, or .png files. 
 
Plural abbreviations 
Don’t add an apostrophe before the s when you form the plural of an abbreviation, unless it’s 
possessive. 
Product names 
Don’t abbreviate any Upwork product names, unless the abbreviation or acronym is commonly 
known and used. View approved product names 
Days and months 
Months and days of the week can be abbreviated where appropriate with no periods. Don't use 
all caps. 
✅ Do this: 
Jan 
Tue 
Mon 
❌Not this: 
JAN 
Tues. 
MON 
Addresses 
U.S. addresses 
For U.S. addresses, put the recipient on the first line, the company (if needed) on the second 
line, the street address on the third line, and then the city/state/postal code on the fourth line.  
Use a comma between the city and state, and include one space between the state and postal 
code. Use “postal code” not “ZIP code.” 
✅ Do this: 
Jose Longoria 
Upwork 
525 W Van Buren St STE 1100 
Chicago, IL 60607 
USA 
U.S. states 
Use AP Style two-letter abbreviation for U.S. state names, such as CA for California. 
International addresses 
Many international addresses put the postal code and the city name on the same line. Put the 
recipient on the first line, the company (if needed) in the second line, the street address on the 
third line, the postal code and the city on the fourth line (which one comes first varies by 
country), and then the country on the fifth line. Include one space between the postal code and 
the city name. 
The format of international addresses varies by country. Use the Universal Postal Union 
guidelines when you format international addresses for specific countries. 
✅ Do this: 
Guillaume Debois 
Accenture 
5 Notre Dame 
13888 Paris 
France 
Capitalization 
There are 3 styles of capitalization: 
■​
Title case capitalizes the first letter of every word except articles, prepositions, and 
conjunctions. This Is Written in Title Case. 
■​
Sentence case capitalizes the first word of each sentence and any proper nouns. 
This is written in sentence case. 
■​
Uppercase capitalizes every letter. THIS IS WRITTEN IN UPPERCASE. 
Here’s how we use them at Upwork. (For specific names of products and features, go to the 
word list.) 
 
Title case 
In general, avoid using title case because: 
■​
It’s harder to process 
■​
It’s slower to read because it uses fewer lowercase letters 
■​
It quickly becomes inconsistent—it’s tricky to decide which words to capitalize 
 
Title case should only be used in these cases: 
●​ Article, blog post, ebook, and success story titles in the Resource Center and blog 
(subheadings remain in sentence case) 
●​ Press releases 
●​ YouTube video titles 
If you need to use it, here’s a helpful text converter. Use the AP style filter.  
 
Sentence case 
We almost always use sentence case. This includes for: 
■​
Headings 
■​
Buttons 
■​
Page titles 
■​
Clickable links 
■​
Menus 
■​
Tooltips 
You’ll need to capitalize the names of brands, companies, software, products, and services — 
these things are proper nouns. 
If you’re not sure what needs to be capitalized, check our word list or send a message to 
#ask-content-design on Slack. 
✅ Do this: 
Your job feed 
View profile 
Try Business Plus 
Go to settings 
❌Not this: 
Your Job Feed 
View Profile 
Try business plus 
Go to Settings 
 
Uppercase (All caps) 
Avoid using all capital letters unless the word is an acronym. It’s hard to read and can come 
across as screaming. 
Exception: "New" and "Beta" tags, which are written in all caps.  
Clause order 
Put conditional clauses before instructions, not after them. Try to mention the circumstance 
before you provide the instructions, so readers can skip the instruction if the condition doesn’t 
apply. 
 
✅ Do this: 
To learn more, see the user guide. 
If you have questions, visit our support guide. 
Now that your first proposal is submitted, are you wondering what’s next? 
 
❌Not this: 
See the user guide to learn more. 
Visit our support guide if you have questions.  
Are you wondering what’s next now that your proposal is submitted? 
Contractions 
Since we want to sound informal and conversational, it’s okay to use contractions when they 
make a sentence easier to understand. 
You can use common contractions of be verbs (also known as state-of-being verbs) and 
auxiliary verbs with not, as well as common contractions of be verbs and auxiliary verbs with 
personal pronouns. 
 
✅ Do this: 
aren’t 
isn’t 
can’t 
couldn’t 
didn’t 
doesn’t 
hadn’t 
haven’t 
weren’t 
won’t 
he’s 
I’m 
I’ve 
it’s 
she’ll 
they’re 
we’ve 
you’re 
here’s 
let’s 
that’s 
there’s 
what’s 
 
Nouns and contractions 
Avoid contractions that combine a proper noun or regular noun with a verb. 
 
✅ Do this: 
Uma will help you draft a proposal. 
Upwork is going to make a big announcement next quarter. 
 
❌ Not this: 
Uma’ll help you draft a proposal. 
Upwork’s going to make a big announcement next quarter. 
Countries and cities 
Use the English version of international countries and cities.  
✅ Do this: 
Sweden 
Germany 
Venice 
❌Not this: 
Sverige 
Deutschland 
Venetia 
Lists 
Use numbers if there are steps the user needs to take in order. Otherwise, use bullets. List 
items should not end with periods, unless the item includes multiple sentences. In that case, 
punctuate normally. 
Here are two examples: 
Numbers 
Here's how to send a proposal: 
1.​ Pick a job 
2.​ Bid your price 
3.​ Set a start date 
4.​ Add the number of hours 
5.​ Send in your proposal 
Bullets 
To bid for a job, you need your: 
■​
Bid amount 
■​
Start date 
■​
Estimate of time it will take to finish 
Quotes 
Always cite the source of a quote or statistic. This includes the author’s last name, year of 
publication, and page number (if applicable). When citing web pages, use the author and date 
if known. 
For example: 
A recent report said "more than 40% of workers would be willing to work from home if it 
meant more time with their family" (Thomas, 2015, 244) 
Long quotes 
If you need to use a long quotation, set off the quotation from the other text using a smaller 
font, with one space before and after the quote. You can also include a pull quote that includes 
part of the quotation. 
URLs 
Don't use the "www" designation (it's obvious by now) and don't include the "http://" path. 
Sub-domains follow these same rules. 
✅ Do this: 
upwork.com 
mail.google.com 
❌Not this: 
http://www.upwork.com 
http://mail.google.com

Heading 
Numbers, dates, and currency 
 
Body copy 
Basics 
Use numerals instead of spelling numbers out. (Exception: for content on the Resource Center 
and blog, spell out numbers below 10 and use numerals for numbers 10 and above.) 
Use a hyphen (-) when indicating a range, and don't put spaces before or after. 
✅ Do this: 
July 18-20 
❌Not this: 
July 18 - 20 
 
Use a comma with numbers of 4 digits or more. 
✅ Do this: 
2,045 
❌Not this: 
2045 
 
Don’t use K for thousands. 
✅ Do this: 
15,000 
❌Not this: 
15K 
Currency 
Use the U.S. dollar for the standard currency. When referring to dollars, use USD instead of 
U.S. Dollar. For foreign currencies, add the currency abbreviation (ISO 4217) after the amount. 
✅ Do this: 
15.50 GBP 
❌Not this: 
15.50 pounds 
 
Spell out million, billion, and other large numbers. 
✅ Do this: 
15 million 
❌Not this: 
15,000,000 
 
Don't use fractional monetary units like cents. 
✅ Do this: 
$0.13 
❌Not this: 
13¢ 
 
Don’t use spaces with numbers and units. 
✅ Do this: 
$15/hr 
❌Not this: 
$15 / hr 
Dates 
Use full dates with a comma after the day when you have the space. Don't add zeroes to days. 
✅ Do this: 
December 3, 2026 
❌Not this: 
Dec 03 2026 
 
Abbreviations 
If you need to shorten the date, use three-letter month abbreviations without periods. Don't use 
shortened formats like MM/DD/YYYY—it doesn't work internationally. 
✅ Do this: 
Dec 3, 2026 
❌Not this: 
12/03/2026 
Jan. 01, 2025 
 
Ordinal numbers 
If you’re including a month, don’t add an ordinal (e.g. th, rd, nd).  
If you’re not including a month, you can include the ordinal. Write it in normal text, not 
subscript. 
✅ Do this: 
This job starts on December 2. 
This job starts on the 12th. 
❌Not this: 
This job starts on December 2nd. 
This job starts on the 12th. 
 
Date ranges 
Separate a range of dates with a hyphen and no spaces before and after. Show the year at 
both the start and end, unless both dates are in the same year. 
✅ Do this: 
December 3, 2025-January 3, 2026 
December 3-10, 2025 
❌Not this: 
December 3, 2025 - January 3, 2026 
December 3 - 10, 2025 
 
Days of the week 
Try not to shorten the days of the week, but if you have to, use Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, 
and Sun. 
Times 
Use numbers, except noon or midnight. If it’s on the hour, you don’t need to include the 
minutes (unless it looks inconsistent with other times you’re talking about). We use the 12-hour 
clock (not 24-hour) and we use am or pm with a space. 
✅ Do this: 
2:30 pm 
4 am 
❌Not this: 
2:30 PM 
03:00 p.m. 
 
Cumulative minutes, hours, elapsed time, and rates 
If you’re talking about cumulated time (like how long a freelancer has worked on a project), use 
decimals without adding zeroes, and use "min" and "hr" to save space. When it comes to 
elapsed time (like timesheets), use a colon between the hours and minutes. 
✅ Do this: 
3.25 hr 
12:31 hr 
60 min meeting 
❌Not this: 
03.25 hours 
12:31 hours 
60 min. meeting 
 
For rates, don't add any spaces. 
✅ Do this: 
$95/hr 
❌Not this: 
$95 / hr 
 
Time ranges 
Separate a range of times with a hyphen and no spaces. Use one lowercase am or pm, and no 
leading zero. 
✅ Do this: 
2-4 pm 
❌Not this: 
02:00 - 04:00PM 
 
Time zones 
Include the user's time zone if it provides helpful context, like when they're scheduling a 
meeting.  
Spell out the time zone when you have space. If not, abbreviate it in all capital letters without 
periods.  
✅ Do this: 
4 pm Australian Eastern Time 
9 am AET 
❌Not this: 
04:00 pacific time 
9:00 am pt 
 
If the time zone doesn’t have a name, use the offset from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). 
Don't include spaces around the plus sign (+) or hyphen (-). 
✅ Do this: 
UTC+7 
UTC-2 
❌Not this: 
4:00 UTC -2 
utc+4 
 
Relative time 
When possible, include the relevant time and date when something happened. 
✅ Do this: 
You hired Chetan on Dec 3, 2016. 
❌Not this: 
You hired Chetan on 852 days ago 
 
Writing dates and times together 
Write the date, then the time and time zone. Use “at to separate them. If it’s less than a week 
away, you can include the day of the week to provide helpful information for the user.  
 
✅ Do this:  
October 20, 2019 at 10:23 am PT 
You have until Friday, February 17, 2023 at 12:30 pm Eastern Standard Time to dispute these 
charges.  
 
❌Not this: 
Oct 20, 2019 10:23 am PT 
You have until February 17, 2023 on Friday, 12:30 pm to dispute these charges.  
 
It’s OK to abbreviate the day of the week and the month. But don’t mix your abbreviations. If 
you’re shortening one, shorten both. 
✅ Do this:  
Your consultation with Haley is on Monday, January 12, 2022 at 1 pm Eastern Standard Time. 
Available on Mon, Jan 12, 2022 at 1 pm ET. 
❌Not this: 
Your consultation with Haley is on Monday, Jan 12, 2022 at 1 pm Pacific Daylight Time.  
 
 
Measurements 
When you’re talking about distance, temperature, volume, size, weight, or any other kind of 
measurement, use numerals (even if it’s under 10).  
You can abbreviate measurements, such as KB and GHz, if the abbreviations are well known. If 
not, spell out the unit in full.  
When using a unit of measurement, put a space between the number and unit. 
✅ Do this: 
128 GB 
❌Not this: 
128GB 
Phone numbers 
International 
For international numbers, or to write U.S. or Canadian numbers for international audiences, 
add the abbreviation of the country and a plus sign (+) immediately before the country code.  
✅ Do this: 
US +1 4155551212 
❌Not this: 
+1 4155551212 
 
Local audiences 
Then you don’t need the country code. For example, if you are writing for a North American 
audience, put the area code in parentheses followed by a space, and then enter a hyphen after 
the three-digit exchange code. 
✅ Do this: 
(415) 555-1212 
❌Not this: 
415-555-1212

Punctuation 
 
Body copy 
Ampersands 
Don’t use an ampersand (&) unless it’s: 
■​
Part of a company or brand name 
■​
Creative use in a headline 
■​
Part of current ontology and structured data practices for category names 
Use "and" since it’s easier to read and will help us localize internationally in the future. Some 
people with lower literacy levels also find ampersands harder to understand.  
✅ Do this: 
Time and payments 
Ben & Jerry's 
❌Not this: 
Time & payments 
Ben and Jerry's 
Apostrophes 
Use the curved apostrophe (‘), the same character as a single closing quotation mark.  
 
Plurals 
Don’t use apostrophes for plurals of single letters, symbols, mathematical signs, or acronyms. 
✅ Do this: 
FAQs 
❌Not this: 
FAQ's 
 
Possessives 
For singular nouns, use an apostrophe followed by an “s”, including proper nouns ending in an 
“s” or “z” sound. For plural nouns, add an apostrophe, but no s. 
✅ Do this: 
The CEO's announcement 
Use the writers' guide 
❌Not this: 
The CEOs announcement 
Use the writers's guide 
 
Don’t use our product names as possessives—rewrite the sentence instead.  
✅ Do this: 
Business Plus makes it easy to find and hire freelancers. 
❌Not this: 
Business Plus’ features make it easy to find and hire freelancers. 
Colons 
Use colons to keep sentences flowing and introduce lists. When you use a colon in a sentence, 
the first word after it should be lowercase. For example: 
Time to get started: let's create your account. 
If the text preceding a colon is bold, make the colon bold as well. 
Commas 
Use commas to separate items in a series, and to separate certain kinds of clauses. Use the 
serial comma to separate items in a list, and place commas (and other punctuation) inside 
quotes.  
✅ Do this: 
Work on websites, apps, and other technology. 
The prototype "works," but not very well. 
❌Not this: 
Work on websites, apps and other technology. 
The prototype "works", but not very well. 
Ellipses 
Use ellipses (...) when you want to indicate an unfinished thought or sentence. They work well 
for loading states. For example: 
Setting up your account... 
Em dashes 
To show a break or a change of direction in the middle of a sentence, use the em dash (—) with 
spaces before or after it. Here's an example. 
See all your apps — work and social — in one place. 
Exclamation points 
Be careful about using exclamation points. Ideally, we want the content of what we're writing to 
excite our users, not the punctuation itself. 
Too many exclamation points can come off as over-excited or self-centered, when we want to 
come off as focused on our users and what they need. 
Hyphens 
Use hyphens to compound adjectives or for ranges. Don't use spaces on either side.  
✅ Do this: 
High-quality talent 
Enter your email address. 
You’ll be working on this project full time.  
 
❌Not this: 
High quality talent 
Enter your e-mail address. 
You’ll be working on this project full-time. 
Parentheses 
Periods in parentheses 
If there is a sentence inside parentheses, the period at the end also goes inside the 
parentheses. In all other cases, the period goes outside the parentheses. For parentheses that 
appear in the middle of sentences, don’t add periods, even if the text in the parentheses is a 
standalone sentence. 
✅ Do this: 
Contact us. (We typically respond in 24 hours or less.) 
❌ Not this: 
Contact us. (We typically respond in 24 hours or less). 
 
Question marks and exclamation points 
Place question marks or exclamation marks inside parentheses only when they’re part of the 
parenthetical text.  
Periods 
End full sentences with periods, unless it's a question, and only leave one space between 
sentences. This includes things like form field descriptions. 
Acronyms and abbreviations 
Don’t use periods with most acronyms or abbreviations, such as MB, PhD, or EPA. Generally, 
it’s better to spell out abbreviations.  
Decimal points 
Use periods to represent decimal points.  
Headings, headlines, and titles 
Don’t use periods in headings, headlines, or titles. This includes things like form field labels. 
In quotes 
In general, put a period inside a quote if it ends a sentence, even if the period isn’t part of the 
text inside the quotation marks. If the text inside quotation marks ends with a question mark or 
exclamation point, don’t use a period. 
Links  
If a link is part of a full sentence, include a period at the end. Make sure the link formatting 
does not include the period (the underline should stop before the period). 
For stand-alone links (e.g., "Show more"), don’t use a period.  
Slashes 
Try not to use slashes. Exceptions include computer files and URLs: then it’s fine. If you’re 
writing out a Windows path, use backslashes (\). 
✅ Do this: 
These instructions are for PC and Mac users. 
❌Not this: 
These instructions are for PC/Mac users. 
Trademarks 
Use the trademark symbol [superscript TM] on the first instance only or with the most prominent 
use of the mark. The remaining instances should be without the symbol. Always use the full 
trademarked name and proper capitalization. You can find a list here.
