# Role guide: Paid Acquisition (requesting ads)

**Who this is for:** anyone on Paid Acquisition who needs ads made. Your whole job in ADAM is
**the order form**. You do not run the tool, drive the gates, or touch Figma. A copywriter and a
designer take it from there.

**Time:** about five minutes per request.

---

## What you're actually doing

You are writing a **request**, not a prompt. ADAM already knows Upwork's brand voice, the legal
rules, the approved claims, and the past ads that performed. What it does *not* know is what this
particular batch is for. That is the only thing you supply.

---

## Filling out the form

Go to the order form and work through three steps.

### 1. Details

- **Requested by.** Your name. It shows on the sprint so people know whose request it is.
- **Audience.** Prospecting, Retargeting, or Both. "Both" produces a separate version of every ad
  for each audience.
- **Delivery date.** When you need them. Minimum five business days out.
- **Deliverable.** Almost always "Images & Copy".
- **Platform.** Meta, LinkedIn, Reddit, YouTube, Google/Bing, or 3rd Party.

**On "Both":** the two versions are genuinely independent ads. They share a look, not a message.
Prospecting talks to people who have never used Upwork, Retargeting to people who already visited.
They do not need to be about the same thing.

### 2. Creative

Pick your **ad styles** and **sizes**. Each style is a different visual layout, and the picker shows
a thumbnail of each. The full catalogue, with how much copy each one actually holds, is further down
this page.

**Ask for about five ads.** This is the single most useful thing you can know. Quality is most
consistent at four to six; past that, concepts start repeating themselves. If you need twelve,
two requests of six will be better than one of twelve.

Bear in mind what you are really asking for: five ads means ADAM writes six concepts for each of
your five styles, narrows to the best, and then produces every size you ticked, for each audience.
Five is already a lot of output.

### 3. Brief: the part that matters

Everything else is a dropdown. **The brief is the only place your judgment enters**, and it is the
highest-priority instruction ADAM gets. It overrides the standing reference docs.

Use the **Insert brief template** button and fill in the four sections:

- **THEME.** One or two sentences. The single core message every ad should lead with.
- **COPY MUST-DOs.** Required phrases, claims to feature, tone notes, things to avoid.
- **DESIGN DIRECTION.** Visual or format cues. Write "N/A" if you have none.
- **RESOURCES.** Links, reference ads, approved stats, badge assets.

**Do not submit a blank brief.** ADAM will warn you, and you can proceed, but you will get generic
evergreen copy written from the standing brand docs with no angle. If you cancel that warning to
go back and add a brief, that works fine. Add it and submit again.

---

## Writing a brief that works

The difference between a good batch and a generic one is almost entirely here. A good brief names a
theme, states what you are testing, and hands over the actual proof points you want used. A weak
brief names a topic and stops.

**Too thin, and you will get evergreen copy:**

> THEME: Hiring on Upwork.
> COPY MUST-DOs: Mention speed.
> DESIGN DIRECTION: N/A
> RESOURCES: N/A

**Enough to work with:**

> THEME: Hiring a specialist should not take six weeks. Lead every ad with the gap between how long
> a traditional hire takes and how fast a client can have someone working on Upwork.
>
> COPY MUST-DOs: Lead with time, not cost. Use "in days, not months" at least once across the set.
> Speak to a hiring manager who has already tried and failed to fill a role. Do not claim a specific
> hire time we have not approved. Avoid "talent pool" and "solutions". No exclamation points.
>
> DESIGN DIRECTION: Prefer the UI and notification styles over lifestyle photography for this batch.
> We want it to feel like the product, not a stock photo.
>
> RESOURCES: [link to the Q3 performance deck], [link to approved stat sheet], the two KOTH ads that
> beat control in July.

Notice what the second one gives ADAM: an angle, a named audience, a phrase to hit, explicit words
to avoid, a format preference, and real source material. Every one of those changes the output.

Two practical notes. **Put claims and stats in RESOURCES**, not from memory, because ADAM will use
what you give it and legal screening runs against the approved reference docs. And if the brief is
for a test, say what the test is. "We are testing whether urgency beats cost" produces a sharper set
than "write urgency ads".

## How much copy actually fits

This is the reference most people want and cannot find. Each style has a real character budget,
enforced by the tool. Picking a style is partly a decision about **how much you get to say**.

If you have a lot to communicate, pick from the high-capacity styles. If your message is one sharp
line, the low-capacity styles will serve it better than a big layout with padding in it.

### Meta ad types (24)

Headline capacity in characters, rounded to what the tool enforces.

- **Split Screen.** A headline above two side-by-side images. Headline only, no body copy. 65 characters, or 49 in the version that carries a CTA.
- **Us vs Them.** Split vertically, illustrated, contrasting two positions. Two headlines at 18 each, plus three bullets a side at 28 each.
- **Photo with Text (Upwork shell).** A branded photo of a professional inside an Upwork border. Headline 24, subhead 43, CTA 19.
- **Lifestyle Photo (Full Bleed).** Photography fills the frame, copy sits over it. Headline 100. The most room of any photo style.
- **Testimonial.** A real client quote with stars or badges. Quote 100, attribution 51. Quotes come from the approved list in the tool.
- **Social Media Profile.** A mockup freelancer profile: headshot, name, job title. Name 18, title 28, two side fields at 60.
- **Pie Chart.** A circular chart in four quadrants. Quadrant labels 30, center callout 28.
- **Hybrid.** Lifestyle photography combined with Upwork UI elements. Headline 36, subhead 60, CTA 22.
- **Search Results.** Three search bars with role tags and hire buttons. Headline 38, each result 32.
- **Text Only.** Text-driven, on a branded background. Headline 20, subhead 60, CTA 22.
- **Chat Bubble.** Two chat bubbles simulating a conversation. Label 18, message 91. Write it as an actual exchange, not two slogans.
- **Notification.** Looks like an app notification. Headline 52, CTA 12.
- **Reminder.** A mobile pop-up reminder. Headline 52, CTA 20.
- **Device UI (Photo).** A phone or laptop showing the Upwork platform. Headline 38.
- **Platform UI.** A featured moment from the product: homepage, search, Uma. Headline 25, subhead 18, CTA 16.
- **Meme.** A viral format carrying brand messaging. Headline 43.
- **Sticky Note.** A note with a title and listed items, single or two columns. Headlines 12 a side, bullets 30.
- **Poll.** A poll bar over a background image. Question 75, two options at 14 each, CTA 12.
- **Tweet / Post Mockup.** Reads like an organic social post. 115 characters. The highest text capacity in the set.
- **Text with Button and Cursor.** A UI ad with a button and a cursor clicking it. Button text 15.
- **Talent Profile.** A freelancer profile card with a headline. Headline 36, CTA 21.
- **Bespoke.** A custom layout with no fixed structure. No enforced caps, and no template, so this one needs design involvement.
- **Illustration.** Illustration tied to the theme. Headline 38, CTA 16.
- **Carousel.** A multi-slide ad. Headline 36, CTA 22.

### Reddit ad types (20)

**Reddit runs tighter than Meta.** Several Reddit types mirror a Meta type at a smaller size, and
the copy budget shrinks with it. If you are ordering the same concept for both platforms, expect the
Reddit version to say less.

- **Meme.** Viral formats with brand messaging. Headline 45.
- **Graphic With Text.** A maze graphic showing the easy path to hiring. Two headlines at 12 each. Very tight.
- **Split Screen.** **Not yet defined.** The spec card for this one is still blank, so caps and fields are unconfirmed. Ask Adrie before ordering it.
- **Pie Chart.** A segmented round graph. Labels 20, center 28. Mirrors Meta Pie Chart with less copy.
- **App Notification.** An inbox icon with a headline call to action. Headline 45.
- **Text Only.** Mostly text. Headline 20, subhead 30, CTA 18. CTA is optional and built in but hidden. Mirrors Meta Text Only with less room.
- **Person With Text.** Text plus a person profile. Headline 30, CTA 15. Similar to Meta Talent Profile.
- **Venn Diagram.** Two overlapping circles. Left and right labels 7 characters each, meaning one word each. Center body 38, subhead 50.
- **Person Only.** A single person, full bleed or avatar style. **No copy on the asset at all.**
- **Us vs Them.** Opposing views on opposite sides. Left headline 22, right headline 18, bullets 60.
- **Testimonial.** A user quote with optional stars or badges. Quote 60, attribution 46. Mirrors Meta Testimonial with less room.
- **Button.** A branded graphic with a button and cursor. Headline 16.
- **Search.** A search bar with variable job title copy. Headline 26.
- **Icon.** An icon and a tagline, calling out a feature or freelancer status. Headline 26.
- **Note.** A reminder note in Upwork brand colors. Headline 60.
- **Notification.** A mobile reminder on a branded background. Headline 52, CTA 20. Mirrors the Meta Reminder type.
- **Search and Checkbox.** A checkbox search UI, with or without a headline. Headline 45, search field 15, list items 12.
- **Text with Icons.** Left-aligned text with icons. Headline 20, list items 8 each.
- **Twitter.** A tweet mockup on a Glimmer background. 115 characters.
- **Logo.** Logo on the brand Glimmer gradient. **No headline or CTA.**

The full per-type rules, including tone, punctuation and CTA policy, live inside the tool and are
applied automatically. You do not need to memorize them. This list exists so you can pick a style
knowing roughly what will fit.

## After you submit

You are done. You will get a sprint link. The copywriter picks it up from there, reviews the copy,
and the designer assembles the finished ads in Figma.

If you want to follow along, the sprint page shows where it is. You do not need to approve anything.

### What the five days are actually for

The minimum is five business days because the work is gated, and the gates are human. A rough shape:

1. **Same day.** The copywriter checks your brief came through intact and starts copy generation.
2. **Day one to two.** Copy review. This is the slowest human step and the one that decides quality.
   Once it is approved, **the copy is frozen**.
3. **Day two to three.** Image direction and image generation. Generation is the slowest machine step.
4. **Day three to five.** Assembly in Figma and final QA.

Asking for a faster turnaround mostly compresses the copy review, which is the step you least want
compressed.

## When to come back

- **The theme was wrong**, or you need a different angle: submit a new request. Copy is locked in
  early, so editing an in-flight sprint is not the path.
- **You need more of the same:** new request, same brief, different styles.
- **Something looks broken:** tell the copywriter running it. They can file it from inside the tool.
- **The copy keeps missing in the same way across batches:** that is worth saying out loud rather
  than re-briefing around it. Recurring copy problems can be fixed permanently in the tool's
  guidance, but only if someone reports the pattern.

## Who to ask about what

- **Copy rules, tone, claims, approved quotes:** Adrie.
- **Templates, layouts, anything about how an ad looks in Figma:** Elise.
- **A sprint that is stuck or errored:** the copywriter running it.
- **A style you want that does not exist yet:** Elise builds the template, then it can be added to
  this form. It is not a same-week request.

---

## The three things worth remembering

1. **The brief is your whole contribution.** Five minutes of real direction there is worth more than
   every other field combined.
2. **Ask for around five.** More is not better, and two smaller requests beat one big one.
3. **Pick styles by how much you need to say.** The character budgets above are real and enforced.
